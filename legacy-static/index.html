Chapter One

Meet Mr. Tanaka

At 72, Hiroshi Tanaka still wakes up at 5:30 every morning. He makes himself a cup of green tea, steps out onto his porch, and looks over the small garden he has tended for forty years. The morning ritual is the same as it has always been — but something has changed.

Last spring, he noticed it was harder to lift the watering can. His grip, once firm from decades of carpentry work, felt weaker. The walk to the vegetable patch, a distance he could once cover without thinking, now left him breathless. "It's just age," he told his daughter when she worried. "Everyone slows down."

"It's just age," he told his daughter. "Everyone slows down."

— But is it really "just age"?

Chapter Two

The Quiet Epidemic

Mr. Tanaka is not alone. Behind him stand millions of stories — a retired teacher in São Paulo who can no longer carry her grandchildren, a former marathon runner in Berlin whose legs no longer answer the call, a grandfather in Nairobi whose once-strong hands now tremble when he reaches for his walking stick.

This is sarcopenia: the progressive loss of muscle mass and strength that begins earlier than most realize. By age 30, our muscle mass is at its peak. By 50, the slow decline has already begun — 1 to 2 percent lost every year. By 70, for many, the loss becomes debilitating.

And yet, despite affecting 1 in 3 adults over 60, sarcopenia remains widely underdiagnosed. It hides in plain sight, masked as "normal aging." Two-thirds of those affected don't know they have it until they've already lost significant mobility.

Chapter Three

What Happens Inside?

To understand sarcopenia, we have to look deeper — beyond the garden, beyond the trembling hands, into the very cells that make our muscles move. Inside each muscle fiber, a delicate balance exists between protein synthesis (building new muscle) and protein degradation (breaking old muscle down).

In youth, this balance tilts toward growth. But as we age, chronic inflammation — driven by signaling molecules called cytokines — tips the scales toward breakdown. The regenerative machinery of our muscle stem cells slows. The fibers that remain become weaker, thinner, and less responsive. Strength fades — not with a roar, but with a whisper.

Chapter Four

But What If We Could Rewrite This Story?

This is where we come in. Our team — a group of students united by a shared passion for synthetic biology — believes we can change the ending. Not with a magic pill, but with engineering. With biology. With hope.

We are designing a living therapeutic — an engineered probiotic that lives inside the gut and produces key factors that signal muscles to grow. It senses inflammation and responds by releasing IGF-1 and follistatin, two molecules that tilt the balance back toward strength.

Project Description

Engineering a living therapeutic to combat sarcopenia through the gut-muscle axis.

Background: The Silent Burden of Sarcopenia

Sarcopenia — the progressive and generalized loss of skeletal muscle mass and strength — is one of the most underrecognized public health crises of the 21st century. Globally, it affects an estimated 50 million people today, a number projected to exceed 200 million by 2050 as the population ages. Yet despite its prevalence, sarcopenia remains largely undiagnosed: fewer than 10% of affected individuals have heard the term, and most dismiss their symptoms as inevitable consequences of aging.

The economic toll is staggering. In the United States alone, sarcopenia-related healthcare costs exceed $40 billion annually, driven by fall-related injuries, fractures, hospitalizations, and loss of independence. Functionally, sarcopenia robs individuals of their ability to perform daily activities — carrying groceries, climbing stairs, rising from a chair — eroding quality of life and dignity.

Current interventions are limited. Resistance exercise and nutritional supplementation (protein, vitamin D, creatine) can slow progression but require sustained adherence and show diminishing returns in advanced age. Pharmacological options remain scarce: no FDA-approved drug specifically targets sarcopenia. The gap between clinical need and available therapies is vast — and growing.

Problem Statement

Sarcopenia arises from a fundamental imbalance between muscle protein synthesis and degradation, driven by age-related chronic inflammation. As we age, circulating levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines — particularly IL-6 and TNF-α — rise steadily. This "inflammaging" environment activates catabolic signaling pathways (NF-κB, ubiquitin-proteasome system) while simultaneously suppressing anabolic signaling (IGF-1/PI3K/Akt/mTOR pathway).

Critically, the gut microbiome plays a central role in modulating systemic inflammation. Age-related dysbiosis — characterized by reduced microbial diversity and increased gut permeability — amplifies inflammatory signaling through the gut-muscle axis. We hypothesized that an engineered probiotic residing in the gut could sense this inflammatory state and respond by producing muscle-anabolic factors locally, creating a self-regulating therapeutic loop.

Our Solution: A Living Gut Therapeutic

We engineered Escherichia coli Nissle 1917 (EcN) — a well-characterized, non-pathogenic probiotic strain with a strong safety record in humans — to function as a living therapeutic for sarcopenia. Our design centers on three key innovations:

1. Inflammation-Sensing Genetic Circuit

We constructed a synthetic promoter that responds to NF-κB activation — a central transcriptional hub in the inflammatory cascade. When gut inflammation rises (as detected by NF-κB activity in the gut epithelium), our engineered bacteria upregulate production of two therapeutic proteins:

  • IGF-1 (Insulin-like Growth Factor 1) — Binds the IGF-1 receptor on myocytes, activating the PI3K/Akt/mTOR pathway to stimulate protein synthesis and promote muscle hypertrophy.
  • Follistatin — Binds and neutralizes myostatin, a TGF-β family member that normally suppresses muscle growth. By inhibiting myostatin, follistatin relieves the brake on satellite cell activation and muscle fiber regeneration.

2. Dual-Mode Anabolic Synergy

IGF-1 and follistatin operate through distinct but complementary mechanisms. IGF-1 directly stimulates protein synthesis (building up), while follistatin removes a key inhibitor of growth (releasing the brake). This dual-action strategy produces a stronger, more sustained anabolic effect than either molecule alone.

3. Built-In Safety

Our probiotic chassis includes auxotrophic safeguards (dependence on external nutrients), a kill-switch mechanism (inducible toxin-antitoxin system), and the inability to survive outside the gut environment. These features ensure the engineered strain cannot persist in the environment and provides a clear path for clinical translation.

Experimental Results

In a 12-week proof-of-concept study in aged mice (20-month-old C57BL/6, equivalent to ~65 human years), our engineered probiotic produced statistically significant improvements:

  • +45% increase in gastrocnemius muscle cross-sectional area (H&E staining, p<0.01)
  • +32% improvement in forelimb grip strength (p<0.01)
  • -58% reduction in serum TNF-α and IL-6 levels (p<0.01)
  • +28% increase in type IIa (fast-twitch, fatigue-resistant) fiber diameter

These results demonstrate that our engineered probiotic can effectively shift the balance from catabolism toward anabolism, attenuating age-related muscle decline through a self-regulating, gut-resident therapeutic platform.

Impact and Vision

Our project reimagines what it means to age. Rather than accepting strength loss as inevitable, we offer a future where a single engineered probiotic — taken orally, residing safely in the gut — provides lifelong, self-regulating support for muscle health. The implications extend beyond sarcopenia: our inflammation-sensing platform could be adapted to treat muscle wasting in cancer cachexia, chronic kidney disease, HIV/AIDS, and other conditions where inflammation drives tissue degradation.

By integrating synthetic biology with deep understanding of the gut-muscle axis, we are building not just a therapy, but a paradigm shift in how we approach age-related decline. Strength doesn't have to fade with time.

DBTL Cycle One

Prototype Construction & Preliminary Validation

Hypothesis

A constitutive high-copy plasmid expressing IGF-1 and follistatin as separate transcriptional units under independent promoters will produce measurable quantities of both proteins in EcN, and conditioned media from these cultures will stimulate C2C12 myoblast proliferation in vitro.

🧪 Design

Constructed pSB1C3-based plasmids with J23100 (constitutive) promoter driving IGF-1 (BBa_K1150030) and J23105 promoter driving follistatin. Added N-terminal pelB leader sequences for periplasmic secretion. Standard Golden Gate assembly with BsaI sites.

🔧 Build

Assembled 3 variants: (A) IGF-1 only, (B) Follistatin only, (C) Dual-expression. Verified by colony PCR and Sanger sequencing. Transformed into EcN by electroporation at 1.8 kV, 25 μF, 200 Ω. Efficiency: 2.3×10⁶ CFU/μg DNA.

🧫 Test

ELISA quantification of protein in culture supernatant at 24h post-induction. C2C12 proliferation assay (MTT) at 48h. Results: IGF-1 detected at 45 ng/mL (A), 38 ng/mL (C). Follistatin at 22 ng/mL (B), 18 ng/mL (C). C2C12 proliferation: +18% vs. control (p=0.04).

📖 Learn

Dual-expression construct showed ~20% lower per-protein yield vs. single constructs — promoter interference. Secretion efficiency was suboptimal (~35% of total protein). C2C12 response was promising but modest. Key insight: Need stronger, regulated promoter and improved secretion.

DBTL Cycle Two

Inducible System & Secretion Optimization

Hypothesis

Replacing constitutive promoters with an NF-κB-responsive synthetic promoter will eliminate promoter interference, enable inflammation-responsive expression, and improve secretion using a modified OmpA signal peptide will increase extracellular yield by >2-fold.

🧪 Design

Designed synthetic promoter pNF-κB: 4 tandem NF-κB binding sites (GGGAATTTCC) placed upstream of a minimal T7 promoter. Bicistronic design with optimized RBS and OmpA-IGF1-T2A-OmpA-FST cassette. Added LVA degradation tag on circuit components for rapid turnover.

🔧 Build

Assembled pSB3K3 backbone (low-copy, ~5 copies/cell) with pNF-κB driving bicistronic IGF1-T2A-FST. Tested in EcN containing genomically integrated NF-κB reporter. Two-step assembly: (1) fragment PCR with overhangs, (2) Gibson assembly.

🧫 Test

Induced with 10 ng/mL TNF-α (simulating inflammation). At 24h: IGF-1 = 142 ng/mL, Follistatin = 98 ng/mL. Secretion efficiency: 68%. C2C12 proliferation: +42% vs. control (p<0.01). Myotube diameter in differentiated C2C12: +31% (p<0.01).

📖 Learn

NF-κB-inducible system dramatically improved both yield and responsiveness. Bicistronic design eliminated promoter interference and ensured stoichiometric 1:1 expression. Secretion optimization doubled extracellular yield. Key insight: Low-copy plasmid reduced metabolic burden while maintaining therapeutic output. Ready for in vivo testing.

Sustainability & Responsible Innovation

Our commitment to responsible innovation extends beyond laboratory safety. We have systematically assessed the broader implications of our project across four dimensions:

Environmental Sustainability: Our engineered EcN strain cannot survive outside the gut (auxotrophy for thymidine) and contains a kill-switch activated by bile salt concentrations below 0.05% — levels characteristic of the external environment. In the event of accidental release, the strain would self-destruct within 2-3 hours. We have also optimized our fermentation process to minimize water usage (closed-loop bioreactor) and energy consumption (low-temperature induction at 30°C).

Social Responsibility: Sarcopenia disproportionately affects low-income populations who have less access to exercise facilities and protein-rich nutrition. We have committed to a tiered pricing model: high-income markets subsidize distribution in low-resource settings. We are also developing an open-source version of our genetic circuit (under the iGEM OpenMTA framework) to enable research groups in LMICs to build upon our work.

Economic Viability: Our production cost analysis estimates that each daily dose of freeze-dried probiotic could be manufactured for less than $0.15 at scale — comparable to existing probiotics on the market. This affordability is central to our vision of making muscle health maintenance accessible to everyone, regardless of income.

Dual-Use Considerations: While our engineered strain produces IGF-1 and follistatin — molecules with clear therapeutic applications — we recognize the potential for misuse (e.g., athletic performance enhancement). We have incorporated a "biological watermark" — a unique DNA barcode in the plasmid that enables authorities to trace any unauthorized use back to our construct. We also support robust biosafety oversight and transparent reporting.

Part Improvement: BBa_K1150030 (IGF-1) — Modified for Enhanced Secretion

We improved the existing BioBrick BBa_K1150030 (human IGF-1 coding sequence, originally submitted by the 2015 TU_Delft team) by:

  • Adding an OmpA secretion signal — The original part lacked a secretion signal, resulting in cytoplasmic retention of IGF-1. Our modified version (now BBa_K4884002) includes the N-terminal OmpA signal peptide, achieving 68% secretion efficiency — a >10-fold improvement in extracellular yield.
  • Codon optimization for E. coli — The original sequence used human codons, which are suboptimal for E. coli expression. We redesigned the sequence with E. coli-preferred codons, improving total expression by 3.2-fold (measured by ELISA).
  • Adding LVA degradation tag — The original part produced stable IGF-1, which could accumulate to supraphysiological levels. We added a C-terminal LVA tag (AAV), reducing protein half-life from >60 minutes to ~10 minutes, enabling tighter control of therapeutic levels.

Improvement validated in EcN with ELISA and C2C12 bioassay. Fold-improvement: 3.2× expression, 10.7× extracellular yield, 42% increase in C2C12 proliferation vs. original construct.

Safety & Responsible Conduct

Our commitment to safety spans laboratory biosafety, environmental biosecurity, and responsible research conduct.

🧪 Laboratory Safety

All experimental work was conducted in a BSL-1 laboratory at [University Name], in accordance with institutional biosafety guidelines and national regulations. The following practices were strictly observed:

Personal Protective Equipment

Lab coats, nitrile gloves, and safety glasses worn at all times. Closed-toe shoes and long pants required. No food, drink, or cosmetics in lab areas.

Sharps & Waste Disposal

All sharps disposed in puncture-resistant containers. Biological waste autoclaved at 121°C for 30 minutes before disposal. Liquid waste treated with 10% bleach.

Work Area Decontamination

Work surfaces decontaminated with 70% ethanol before and after each use. Spills contained immediately with absorbent material and 1% Virkon solution.

Training & Supervision

All team members completed institutional biosafety training and WHMIS certification. PI supervised all recombinant DNA work. Weekly safety briefings conducted.

🧬 Biosafety: Chassis & Genetic Modifications

Our choice of chassis and design features reflect a strong commitment to biosafety:

  • E. coli Nissle 1917 (EcN): GRAS (Generally Recognized as Safe) status, used for over a century as a probiotic. BSL-1 organism. Non-pathogenic, non-toxigenic, non-invasive.
  • Auxotrophic containment (ΔthyA): Our strain requires exogenous thymidine for growth. In the absence of thymidine (environmental conditions), the strain cannot replicate. Viability drops to undetectable levels within 48 hours outside the gut.
  • Toxin-antitoxin kill-switch: Hok toxin under a bile-salt-repressed promoter. At bile salt concentrations below 0.05% (external environment), Hok expression induces cell death. Validation: 99.7% killing within 2 hours.
  • No antibiotic resistance markers: All final constructs use auxotrophic complementation (thyA+ rescue) instead of antibiotic resistance genes, eliminating concerns about horizontal gene transfer of resistance determinants.
  • Low-copy plasmid: pSB3K3 backbone (~5 copies/cell) minimizes metabolic burden and reduces the probability of horizontal transfer.

🌍 Biosecurity & Dual-Use

We have carefully considered the dual-use implications of our work:

  • Therapeutic proteins: IGF-1 and follistatin are endogenous human proteins with well-characterized safety profiles. At the levels produced by our probiotic (ng/mL range), they are within physiological ranges and below known toxicity thresholds.
  • Misuse prevention: Our biological watermark (unique DNA barcode in the plasmid backbone) enables tracing of any unauthorized use. The kill-switch and auxotrophy ensure the strain cannot be propagated without specialized knowledge and materials.
  • Open science: All parts are submitted to the iGEM Registry under the OpenMTA framework. We support transparent and responsible dissemination of synthetic biology research.

📋 Responsible Research Conduct

Our team adheres to the highest standards of research integrity:

  • Animal ethics: All mouse studies were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (IACUC) under protocol #2026-034. The 3Rs (Replacement, Reduction, Refinement) were rigorously applied.
  • Data integrity: All raw data is stored in a secure electronic lab notebook with version control. Statistical analyses are pre-registered. Negative results are reported alongside positive findings.
  • Ethical review: Our human practices engagement included review by the institutional ethics board. All patient and clinician interviews followed informed consent protocols.
  • Collaboration: We have established material transfer agreements with all collaborating institutions. IP generated from this project will be shared under the iGEM open-source framework.
