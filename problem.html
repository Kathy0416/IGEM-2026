The Slow Fade of Strength

Sarcopenia is a progressive loss of muscle mass and function that begins earlier than most realize. By age 30, our muscles are at their peak — but by 50, the quiet decline has already begun, stealing 1–2% of our strength every year. Despite affecting hundreds of millions globally, it remains vastly underdiagnosed and undertreated.

Peak Mass

Age 30

Muscle mass reaches its natural peak. From this point onward, without intervention, a gradual decline in both fiber number and cross-sectional area begins.

Onset

Age 50

Gradual decline accelerates — 1–2% loss per year. Muscle protein synthesis slows while inflammatory cytokines like IL-6 and TNF-α begin to rise.

Debility

Age 70

Significant functional impairment affects daily life. Loss of mobility, increased fall risk, and reduced independence become major quality-of-life concerns.

1 in 3 adults over 60 are affected by sarcopenia worldwide. That's over 200 million people — and the number is rising as the global population ages.
$40B+ annual healthcare burden in the US alone, driven by fall-related injuries, hospitalizations, and long-term care costs linked to muscle weakness.
67% of patients remain undiagnosed until significant mobility loss has already occurred. Sarcopenia is often dismissed as "normal aging."
