Guided by Community

We engage with clinicians, patients, ethicists, and policymakers to ensure our work is responsible and relevant.

We began by interviewing geriatric clinicians who told us that sarcopenia is one of the most underdiagnosed conditions in their practice — nearly 40% of patients over 70 meet the diagnostic criteria, yet fewer than 10% have ever heard the term. We then spoke with elderly patients themselves, who shared how the loss of strength had quietly stolen their independence: the inability to carry groceries, to play with grandchildren, to rise from a chair without help. These conversations transformed our understanding of the problem from a biological challenge into a deeply human one.

Guided by safety experts and synthetic biology ethicists, we refined our probiotic chassis to include auxotrophic safeguards and a kill-switch mechanism, ensuring the engineered strain cannot persist outside the controlled environment of the gut. We also consulted with regulatory advisors to map a responsible pathway from bench to bedside, considering the unique challenges of bringing a living therapeutic to market. Every design iteration was shaped by this dialogue — our project is not just something we built for the community, but with them.

— Team iGEM 2026, Human Practices Lead
